Lithe compiler speed program

Change & safety record · branch recur/compiler-speed-program @ aec6d0d

Goal: make the Lithe compiler dramatically faster without weakening any security property of the language or the compiler. Result: Release builds 1.55× faster single-threaded and 3.21× at 16 threads; the new --fast mode is 10.2× faster end-to-end; LSP keystroke latency fell from ~2,400 ms to 6.2 ms. Safety: release bytecode +0.08 % against a 1 % budget, transaction gas +0.0022 %, and all 1,786 replayed transaction outcomes byte-for-byte unchanged.

1. Where the time went — and what changed

The original problem was structural: every compile ran the entire emit → promote → optimize → outline → assemble pipeline 50–100 times, hill-climbing over codegen plans to pick the cheapest by gas + size. That search was ~99 % of wall time. Three changes fixed it.

Three of §4's headline targets were not met. All three are recorded with their measurements; two were explicitly accepted. Nothing was quietly dropped or redefined after the fact.

Why the two accepted misses are structural, not sloppy

LSP ≤ 5 ms. The server already does the minimum: no codegen, lazy index, lints reuse the checked units. The remaining 6.2 ms is check itself at 5.3–5.8 ms. Getting under the bar would mean making the type checker faster for speed's sake, which the invariants forbid — so the correct engineering answer is to accept the miss rather than erode a safety-critical component.

Ladder ≤ 25 ms. The remaining time is optimize and outline on code whose bytes are pinned by the snapshot and the 1 % budget. Two candidate cuts were measured and rejected: a per-round incremental flow costs 10–12 % of a loop that is itself a small part of the total, and merge_tails already takes every free pair per call.

4. Also evaluated and deliberately not done

Each of these was measured and rejected on the numbers, not on intuition. The measurements are in PROGRESS.md so a reviewer can re-run them.

CandidateWhy not
4.1 Per-function parallel emitPer-body emission is only 9.1 % of the Fast backend and 8.2 % of Release, so even with unlimited threads and a free join the cap is 1.10×. The join would also have to relocate shared emitter state, including 21 cases that currently give up.
5.5 Index-arena ASTCosts 1,378 compile errors across 41 files for 0.08 % of a Release build and 1.2–1.7 % of a Fast one. The dominant cost is Program.modules, which a flat arena does not make cheaper.
3.3 Value-numbering stack schedulerpromote is 8.9 % of the Fast backend over bench/ — it moves no open target. The batched promotion that did pay was landed instead as 3.3b(ii).
6.1–6.3 Startup, assembler, keccakMeasured; no win large enough to justify the churn. The assembler was already a single fixed-point pass and candidate cost was already cached.
IndexMap / new cratesNot needed: the two order-sensitive maps are BTreeMaps. Still one dependency.
